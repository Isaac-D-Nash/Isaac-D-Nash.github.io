Momentum

Overview

The first game I ever worked on — a 3D platformer built from scratch in Unity, with jumping, wall running, grappling, and secret areas hidden through the levels.

Momentum is set inside a volcano. Players take the role of Craig's soul, trying to escape the torment of the hell he found himself in for the deeds he committed in life. Hit an obstacle, or the floor, and you start the level over — I've watched people take ten minutes or more to beat a single level, getting a little better on each run.

Design and build

I built the platforming systems myself — jumping, wall running, and grappling all needed to feel tight and responsive, since the whole game is built around precision movement.

Each level started as a rough sketch of what I wanted, then most of my time went into whiteboxing — making sure it flowed properly while staying genuinely difficult to complete. I wanted each level a little longer and harder than the last, introducing new mechanics like the grappling hook as the game progressed. Once a level held up for me and for playtesters, I'd texture it and do a final pass to make sure everything still flowed.

Challenges and solutions

I was working on a mediocre laptop at the time, so my optimisation skills had to be off the charts — every system needed to run smoothly without expensive hardware to fall back on.

To keep performance in check, I kept the detail in each level to a minimum needed to still read well, so even lower-spec laptops could run it. It was my first project, and the discipline it taught me carried straight into everything I've built since.

What I learned

Playtesting taught me that I'm never going to get it right on the first try, because people will always find bugs you didn't expect.

One tester got hit by an obstacle and was launched clean off the level — the knockback was clearly tuned too high, so I brought it down. I played through each level myself looking for bugs until I couldn't find any more, then handed it to a small group of testers, who promptly found several I'd missed.
